            What powers this

              The flood level for each road is an estimate until the township's drain and flood records are connected. The route cost is a simple model, not a measured one. Weather forecasts cover a few kilometres per grid square, so they describe the area around your walk, not each street.

              How we estimate flooding
              1. Terrain. Each road's height comes from Open-Meteo elevation (about 90 m resolution). A road that sits lower than the roads around it collects water first.
              2. Water nearby. Streams, rivers and drains mapped in OpenStreetMap lower a road's threshold the closer it is to them.
              3. The threshold. Each road gets a rainfall rate (mm/h) at which we expect water on the road. The warning compares that with the rain forecast for the hours of your walk.
              4. Resident reports. A blocked-drain report lowers that road's threshold to 60% of normal (an assumption). Other reports raise its place in the township queue. When the team marks it cleared, the normal value returns.

              What this is not. It is an estimate, not a flood map. Public services do not publish drain capacity or past flood records. Connecting the township's own records, and checking the estimate against where water really collected, is the first step of a pilot.
